2. Provisional filing gate

The project's provisional-filing rule is substantively correct and should remain a hard project-control rule.

Under 35 U.S.C. 111(b), a provisional does not require a claim satisfying §112(b)-(e), but it does require a specification prescribed by §112(a) and any required drawing. 5. Where I would search for the invention now

The useful question is no longer simply whether “AI recognizes a pig and then kills it” is patentable. Earlier art makes that proposition too broad.

The more promising technical distinctions to investigate are:

Persistent local-population membership modeling: establishing a roster or probabilistic representation of a particular sounder rather than merely recognizing the species or counting animals currently visible.

Completeness determination: deciding that a prescribed population-completeness criterion has been met across observations, identities, confidence levels, temporal windows, and sensor coverage.

Actuation conditioned on population state: the lethal mechanism remains unavailable until the group-state criterion is satisfied, with abort/re-arm behavior if an expected individual disappears, an unknown animal appears, or sensor confidence degrades.

Selective timing/access with nonselective toxicity: making clear that selectivity lies in access, dispensing, authorization, temporal gating, or exposure rather than falsely suggesting that the poison itself selectively affects pigs.

Safety and auditable decision architecture: non-target exclusion, conflicting-modality handling, confidence hysteresis, human override, fail-safe states, event logs, post-actuation records, and rules for uncertainty.

Whether those distinctions are actually novel remains an empirical search question. They are simply better candidates for investigation than the current generic Claim 1 architecture.

Correct framing

A provisional application does not require a formal patent claim. 35 U.S.C. §111(b)(2) expressly provides that claims under §112(b)-(e) are not required, and USPTO likewise states that a provisional may be filed without a formal patent claim. (USPTO)

So our working 3-independent + 17-dependent Set 1 is not a limitation on the provisional. It remains useful for:

testing whether the disclosure supports contemplated future scope;

identifying §112 gaps;

prior-art searching;

designing a broad-to-narrow nonprovisional claim ladder;

anticipating restriction issues.

It is not a provisional filing requirement or a disclosure ceiling.

Important new-matter distinction

The part I would phrase slightly differently is:

We want to disclose as much of the reasonably contemplated invention now as possible so that later claims can be added or changed without requiring subject matter that was absent from the original disclosure.

That is different from saying that the provisional permits us to add new material later without it becoming new matter.

USPTO states that a filed provisional generally cannot be substantively amended after it receives its filing date. Matter absent from the specification, claims, and drawings at filing is ordinarily new matter if later introduced. (USPTO)

For a later nonprovisional claim to receive the provisional's date, the provisional must adequately describe and enable the subject matter of that later claim under §112(a). The fact that no claims were filed in the provisional does not relax that requirement. (USPTO)

So our operative rule becomes:

Broad disclosure now → freedom to formulate different claims later.

Not:

Broad disclosure now → permission to introduce undisclosed technical subject matter later.

Supplemental provisional strategy

There is also a useful mechanism for developments during the 12-month period.

If we conceive a materially new implementation after Provisional A is filed, we should not attempt to amend Provisional A. Instead, we can consider filing Provisional B containing the new material.

A later nonprovisional may claim the benefit of one or more prior-filed provisional applications under 37 C.F.R. §1.78, provided the applicable requirements are satisfied. (USPTO)
