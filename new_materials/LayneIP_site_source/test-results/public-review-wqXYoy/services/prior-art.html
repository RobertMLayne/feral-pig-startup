Prior-art research & patentability

Search the technical record and make the relationship between a disclosure and a proposed claim explicit.

LAYNE / IP
← All services

Useful research makes both the evidence and the limits of the search visible.

A search is most useful when it begins with a defined technical question. The research plan identifies concepts, alternative terminology, classifications, and the kinds of patent and non-patent literature most likely to matter.

Results are organized at the feature or limitation level, with source locators and a record of the search strategy. Analysis distinguishes what a reference actually discloses from what would require an inference or additional evidence.

What the work can include

  • Patent and non-patent literature searches
  • Feature and claim-limitation mapping
  • Search strategies, classifications, and terminology
  • Novelty and nonobviousness analysis for prosecution
  • Technical comparison of potentially relevant disclosures
  • Source provenance and reproducible search notes

Typical work product

  • Search report and reference library index
  • Source-supported comparison matrix
  • Gaps, assumptions, and follow-up questions

Engagement scope

No search can guarantee that every relevant reference has been found. Patentability analysis does not establish freedom to operate.
